The one idea: across and up

Everything sits on a grid, and everything uses the same two numbers. across = 0 is the far left; up = 0 is the bottom row — just like a graph in maths class. Pick a brick, click a square, and watch the Python write itself.

76543210
01234567

Click an empty square to place · click a filled one to remove it. This is exactly what a kid does in their editor, one line at a time.


    

  






  

    
THE SIX BRICKS

    
Every game is these six, mixed differently

    
Catch, chase, maze, platformer, puzzle — same six pieces, different settings. Learn them once and the second game is almost free.

    

      
01 · WHO YOU ARE
The player
hero = game.player(
    shape="hero",
    control="arrows")
arrows · wasd · mouse · arrows+jump

      
02 · HOW THINGS MOVE
Movement
game.ghost(across=4, up=6,
    move="chases")
still · falls · drifts · chases · bounces

      
03 · AGAIN AND AGAIN
A loop
@game.every(1)
def drop():
    game.drop("star")
Every 1 second, a star falls in.

      
04 · WHEN TWO THINGS TOUCH
A rule
@game.when_touch(hero, "star")
def caught(star):
    game.score += 1
burst() · say("hi") · grow() · remove()

      
05 · WHAT YOU KEEP
Score & lives
game.score += 1
game.lives -= 1
game.hearts(3)
Extra lives appear as you play. You choose how many.

      
06 · HOW YOU WIN
One clear goal
game.win_when(
  game.collected_all("coin"))
score_reaches · survived · reached("flag")

    

  






  

    
DRAW A LEVEL WITH YOUR KEYBOARD

    
What you type is what you see

    
One character per square, right way up. Redraw the picture and the level changes — no coordinates to count at all.

    

      
game.level("""
    #########
    #@..#..c#
    #.#.#.#.#
    #.#...#.#
    #.###.#g#
    #c..#.#.#
    ##.##.#c#
    #########
""")

# several pictures become levels —
# win one and the next loads itself
game.levels(one, two, three)

📐 Maths, without calling it maths

        
    
          
  • Cartesian coordinates — across/up from the bottom-left is a graph
    • 
          
  • Counting from zero, made concrete instead of abstract
    • 
          
  • 2D grids — the level picture is an array they wrote by hand
    • 
          
  • Rates & estimation — "can I jump 3 squares to reach up=6?"
    • 
        

      

      

        
🧠 Computational thinking

        
    
          
  • Decomposition — every game is the same six bricks
    • 
          
  • Build–test–fix — put the flag out of reach and nothing errors. They play, find it impossible, redesign
    • 
          
  • Cause and effect — speed=8 vs speed=3 is hypothesis testing
    • 
        

      

      

        
💛 And the part that decides if they continue

        
    
          
  • Authorship — it's their level, not a worksheet
    • 
          
  • Failure is playable, not punitive
    • 
          
  • Designing for someone else — handing a level to a sibling and watching them fail

FOR TEACHERS & PARENTS

    
You don't need to know games

    

      

        
A 40-minute session

        
    
          
  • 5′ Play a ready-made game. Don't open the file yet.
    • 
          
  • 10′ Change three numbers — predict out loud first, then run.
    • 
          
  • 10′ Draw their own level picture.
    • 
          
  • 10′ Make it fair — tune hearts, speed, jump power.
    • 
          
  • 5′ Swap seats and play each other's game.
    • 
        

      

      

        
When they're stuck, say this

        
    
          
  • “Run it and show me what happens.”
    • 
          
  • “What did you expect? What happened instead?”
    • 
          
  • “Read the message out loud — it tells you the fix.”
    • 
          
  • “Change one number and run it again.”
    • 
        

        
Don't fix their file for them. Ask them to describe the square they want, and let them find the numbers.

      

    

    

      

        
💬 Errors that teach instead of scare

        
Oops — I don't know the shape 'basktet'.
Try one of these: hero, basket, ship, star...
(did you mean 'basket'?)

      

      

        
🚧 What this doesn't teach

        
Writing their own classes, files, algorithms, recursion, testing, version control. This is the on-ramp. The step out is a kid outgrowing hearts() and writing their own spawn logic with free_square().

      

    

  






  

    
GET STARTED

    
Three commands

    

      
1 · Play
$ pip install gamebricks
$ gamebricks maze
A real window opens. Arrow keys work.

      
2 · Get the code
$ gamebricks copy
Seven example games land in their own folder, ready to edit. Running it twice never overwrites their work.

      
3 · Start fresh
$ gamebricks new my_game.py
A small blank game with # <-- CHANGE THESE markers to build on.

    

    
Python 3.12+. One dependency. Works on Windows, macOS and Linux.
